1 · A brief history

  • 1930s–40s — Vittorio Erspamer identifies an indole from gastric mucosa he calls enteramine; meanwhile Rapport, Green & Page isolate the serum vasoconstrictor from platelets and name it serotonin. Rapport shows both are 5-hydroxytryptamine. [1]
  • 1953 — Twarog & Page detect serotonin in mammalian brain — the start of central 5-HT neurochemistry. [2]
  • 1957 — Gaddum & Picarelli distinguish two tryptamine receptors (the "M" and "D" receptors — forerunners of 5-HT3 and 5-HT2), while imipramine (Kuhn, 1957) and iproniazid enter psychiatry as the first modern antidepressants, later shown to act on 5-HT transmission. [3]
  • 1964 — Dahlström & Fuxe map the central 5-HT neurons to the raphe nuclei (groups B1–B9) with the Falck–Hillarp histofluorescence technique. [4]
  • 1966–70 — Koe & Weissman show p-chlorophenylalanine selectively depletes brain serotonin [5]; Aghajanian and colleagues record the first single-unit activity of identified raphe serotonergic neurons [7]; and tryptophan hydroxylase is purified and characterized as a BH4-dependent enzyme [6].
  • 1974–75 — Wong, Horng, Bymaster, Hauser & Molloy at Eli Lilly report Lilly 110140 (fluoxetine), a new selective inhibitor of serotonin uptake into rat brain synaptosomes — the birth of the SSRI era. [8]
  • 1979 — Peroutka & Snyder, with radioligand binding, demonstrate multiple serotonin receptors — the founding act of 5-HT receptor subtyping. [9]
  • 1988–91 — The 5-HT1A receptor is cloned (the G-21 genomic clone) [10], and Maricq and colleagues clone and express the 5-HT3 receptor, proving it is a ligand-gated ion channel. [11]
  • 1993–95 — The molecular-genetic era: Brunner's MAO-A kindred with impulsive aggression [12], the 5-HT1B knockout mouse with elevated aggression [13], the 5-HT7 receptor cloning [14], and the MAO-A knockout mouse [15].
  • 1996–98 — The 5-HTTLPR transporter polymorphism is linked to anxiety traits [16]; the 5-HT1A knockout mouse is created as an anxiety model [17].
  • 2002–05 — Gene × environment: MAO-A genotype moderates the effect of maltreatment on antisocial behaviour [18] and 5-HTTLPR moderates life-stress effects on depression [19]; a second, neuron-specific tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH2) is discovered [20], soon tied to major depression by a loss-of-function mutation [21].

2 · Metabolism and the enzymes of the pathway

5-HT is synthesised from the essential amino acid L-tryptophan in just two steps, and inactivated by oxidative deamination and/or reuptake. The full pathway, including the melatonin branch in the pineal gland, is:

Enzymes

Tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH1 & TPH2)

The rate-limiting, BH4-dependent hydroxylation of tryptophan at C-5 [6]. Walther and colleagues discovered a second, neuron-specific isoform: TPH2 builds brain 5-HT, while TPH1 builds the peripheral pool [20]. A TPH2 loss-of-function mutation was identified in unipolar major depression [21].

Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC/DDC)

A pyridoxal-5′-phosphate-dependent enzyme that decarboxylates 5-HTP to 5-HT (and L-DOPA to dopamine); shared between indoleamine and catecholamine pathways and not rate-limiting. Inherited AADC deficiency causes a combined monoamine neurotransmitter disorder with hypotonia, oculogyric crises and autonomic dysfunction.

Monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A)

The principal catabolic enzyme: oxidative deamination of 5-HT to 5-hydroxyindoleacetaldehyde, then aldehyde dehydrogenase to 5-HIAA. A nonsense MAO-A mutation in a Dutch kindred produced impulsive aggression (Brunner syndrome) [12]; MAO-A knockout mice are similarly aggressive [15]; and MAO-A genotype moderates the effects of childhood maltreatment on antisocial behaviour [18].

Pineal branch enzymes

5-HT is also the precursor of melatonin: AANAT (arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase, the nocturnally norepinephrine-induced circadian gateway enzyme) and ASMT/HIOMT (N-acetylserotonin methyltransferase). This is why serotonin is the metabolic parent of the pineal sleep hormone.

Note: more than 90% of the body's 5-HT is peripheral (gut enterochromaffin cells; platelets take up but do not synthesize it); brain 5-HT is a separate, small pool synthesized in situ behind the blood–brain barrier — the TPH1/TPH2 division formalizes this [20].

3 · Anatomy: the serotonergic raphe system

All forebrain serotonin arises from ~20,000–30,000 neurons clustered along the midline/paramedian brainstem — the raphe nuclei, divided by Dahlström & Fuxe (1964) into cell groups B1–B9 [4]. The definitive modern neuroanatomical account is Jacobs & Azmitia's Physiological Reviews monograph [22].

Nucleus (group)LocationMajor projections & functional notes
Raphe pallidus / obscurus (B1, B2)MedullaSpinal cord (descending pain modulation, autonomic tone, respiratory control; serotonergic defects implicated in sudden infant death syndrome).
Raphe magnus (B3)MedullaDorsal horn of spinal cord — descending analgesia; 5-HT effects on nociceptive transmission.
Dorsal raphe nucleus (DRN) (B6, B7)Midbrain (largest 5-HT cluster)Cortex, striatum, thalamus, amygdala, hippocampus — mood, arousal, sensory gating, inhibition of punished/aversively controlled behaviour; the principal target through which SSRIs act.
Median raphe nucleus (MnR) (B5, B8)Midbrain/pontineHippocampus and forebrain — complementary to DRN; hippocampal 5-HT arises roughly half from MnR; theta-rhythm and anxiety-related circuits.
Raphe pontis / centralis superior (B4, B8)PonsCerebellum, brainstem; reticulospinal modulation.
Rostral linear / caudal linear nuclei (B8, B9)Ventral midline near interpeduncular nucleusForebrain projections overlapping with DR/MnR; part of the dorsal raphe–ventral tegmental interface relevant to reward.

Physiology: serotonergic neurons fire slowly and regularly (~1–5 Hz); Aghajanian's group showed they are exquisitely sensitive to LSD and related indoleamines, which depressed raphe firing at doses producing behavioral effects [7]. They are inhibited by 5-HT1A somatodendritic autoreceptors and 5-HT1B terminal autoreceptors — the mechanism exploited by buspirone and underlying SSRIs' delayed therapeutic onset.

4 · Receptor subtypes and behavioural correlates

With the exception of 5-HT3 (a cation channel [11]), all mammalian 5-HT receptors are G-protein-coupled. Fourteen human subtypes are grouped into seven families; the standard nomenclature references are Hoyer et al. 2002 [23] and Nichols & Nichols 2008 [24].

SubtypeTransductionKey localisationsBehavioural / clinical correlations (representative evidence)
5-HT1AGi/o ↓ cAMP; opens GIRK K+ Raphe (autoreceptor), hippocampus, cortex, amygdala Anxiety & depression: the −1019C/G promoter polymorphism derepresses 5-HT1A autoreceptor expression and is associated with depression and suicide [25]; 5-HT1A knockout mice are anxiogenic in the open field and elevated-plus maze [17]; agonists such as buspirone and 8-OH-DPAT are anxiolytic/antidepressant in animals and humans.
5-HT1BGi/o Basal ganglia, nucleus accumbens, terminals (autoreceptor) Impulsivity & aggression: 5-HT1B knockout mice show markedly increased aggression in resident-intruder tests [13]; the receptor has been associated with ADHD, OCD and addictions in human studies.
5-HT1D/1E/1FGi/oSubstantia nigra, cortex, trigeminal system Triptan antimigraine drugs act at 5-HT1B/1D; 5-HT1F-selective agonists (lasmiditan) abort migraine without vascular 5-HT1B agonism.
5-HT2AGq/11 ↑ IP3 Cortical layer V, claustrum, platelets Psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin) act as 2A agonists; 2A blockade contributes to atypical antipsychotics (clozapine). LSD's profound effects on raphe firing and behaviour initiated the modern 5-HT-psychedelia literature [7].
5-HT2BGq/11Gut, heart valves, pulmonary vessels Valvulopathy: 5-HT2B agonism explains fenfluramine-induced cardiac valve disease [26].
5-HT2CGq/11 Hypothalamus, choroid plexus, VTA Appetite & reward; RNA editing of the 2C receptor changes receptor coupling and is associated with depression and suicide [27]; 2C antagonism causes antipsychotic-induced weight gain.
5-HT3Ligand-gated Na+/K+ channel Area postrema, nucleus tractus solitarius, hippocampal interneurons, gut Emesis: the receptor is a serotonin-gated ion channel [11]; antagonists (ondansetron, granisetron) are antiemetics in chemotherapy-induced nausea.
5-HT4Gs ↑ cAMP Hippocampus, striatum, gut myenteric plexus Cognition & memory facilitation (agonists enhance hippocampal transmission) and gut prokinesis; see the classic 5-HT4 review by Boess & Martin [28].
5-HT5AGi/o Hippocampus, cortex Least characterised; suggested roles in circadian rhythm and locomotor activity.
5-HT6Gs ↑ cAMP Striatum, hippocampus, cortex Cognition: antagonists improve performance in rodent memory tasks; trials in Alzheimer's disease.
5-HT7Gs ↑ cAMP Hypothalamus, hippocampus, suprachiasmatic nucleus Cloned as a novel adenylyl-cyclase-activating receptor implicated in mood disorders [14]; circadian phase shifting and sleep architecture.

Transporters complete the picture: SERT (SLC6A4) clears synaptic 5-HT — the site of fluoxetine's SSRI action, discovered by Wong and colleagues at Eli Lilly [8] — and the 5-HTTLPR promoter polymorphism was linked to anxiety-related traits [16] and later to life-stress moderation of depression [19] (a finding still under meta-analytic debate [29]).

5 · Lesion and depletion studies

Pharmacological depletion

  • p-Chlorophenylalanine (PCPA) — the classic TPH inhibitor, described by Koe & Weissman as a specific depletor of brain serotonin [5]. It depletes brain 5-HT by >90% and produces insomnia (Jouvet's school: Mouret & Jouvet, 1968) — the founding evidence that serotonin is a sleep-promoting neurotransmitter; it also alters pain sensitivity and anxiety-related behaviour.
  • Acute tryptophan depletion (ATD) — Delgado, Charney, Price, Aghajanian and Heninger showed that a tryptophan-free amino-acid drink transiently reverses antidepressant response in remitted depressed patients, tying SSRI efficacy to 5-HT synthesis in humans [30].
  • MDMA / fenfluramine — 5-HT releasers producing long-lasting serotonergic terminal deficits in animals; distal axotomy of 5-HT neurons is the established substrate of MDMA neurotoxicity (O'Hearn, Molliver, Ricaurte and colleagues) [31].

Neurotoxic and genetic lesions

  • 5,7-Dihydroxytryptamine (5,7-DHT) — a SERT-taken-up neurotoxin that selectively destroys serotonergic terminals; combined with desipramine pretreatment (to protect noradrenergic neurons), it is the standard tool for mapping 5-HT terminal fields.
  • 6-Hydroxydopamine contrast: lesioning catecholamine neurons while sparing 5-HT neurons established the pathway specificity of both neurotoxins.
  • Electrolytic/excitotoxic raphe lesions — dorsal raphe lesions reduce forebrain 5-HT and alter anxiety, REM sleep and drug self-administration; median raphe lesions impair hippocampal-dependent behaviours.
  • Genetic "lesions" of the phenotype — the Pet-1 ETS transcription factor is a precise marker and determinant of 5-HT neuron development: Pet-1-null mice lose most central 5-HT neurons [32]; conditional Lmx1b deletion in 5-HT neurons eliminates the central serotonergic system (with normal locomotor activity) [33] — and these mice lose behavioural responses to SSRIs, defining 5-HT neurons as necessary for SSRI action.
