The Experimental Path to DNA Delivery

Each milestone below was a "forced entry" experiment: kill, starve or shock cells and see if external heritable material gets in. Together they proved DNA is the genetic material and turned DNA delivery into a routine tool. Click a milestone to expand.

1928 — Griffith: "transforming principle" in Streptococcus pneumoniae GENETICS
Heat-killed virulent (smooth) pneumococci mixed with live avirulent (rough) cells made the live cells virulent. Something from dead cells entered live cells and transformed them — the first evidence of horizontal gene transfer, though Griffith didn't know it was DNA.
Why it matters: proved bacteria can take up and express external hereditary material.
DOI: 10.1017/S0022172400031879 · PubMed record
1944 — Avery, MacLeod & McCarty: the transforming principle is DNA LANDMARK ★
Purified the transforming principle from pneumococci and destroyed protein, RNA and DNA selectively with enzymes. Only DNase destroyed transforming activity → DNA carries hereditary information and can cross the cell envelope of naturally competent bacteria.
DOI: 10.1084/jem.79.2.137 · PubMed record · J Exp Med 79:137–158
1952 — Hershey & Chase: ³²P-labeled phage DNA, not ³⁵S protein, enters cells CONFIRMATION ★
Blender experiment: only the DNA (³²P) injected by T2 phage entered E. coli; the protein shell (³⁵S) stayed outside. DNA entry into cells via a syringe-like mechanism — a natural, evolved DNA delivery device.
DOI: 10.1084/jem.96.4.425 · PubMed record
1958 — Spizizen: Bacillus subtilis becomes the genetic workhorse via natural competence
Showed auxotrophic B. subtilis strains can be transformed with naked DNA when starved, founding bacterial genetics in a genetically tractable Gram-positive.
DOI: 10.1073/pnas.44.10.1072 · PubMed record
1970 — Mandel & Higa: CaCl₂ shock makes E. coli permeable to DNA METHOD ★
Exposure to ice-cold CaCl₂ made E. coli "competent" for purified λ phage DNA uptake — the birth of artificial chemical transformation. The mechanism remains partly unclear: Ca²⁺ shields the negative charges of DNA and membrane, destabilizes the LPS layer, and may create transient membrane pores during the heat shock.
DOI: 10.1016/0022-2836(70)90083-3 · PubMed record · J Mol Biol 53:159–162
1972–1973 — Cohen, Boyer & Hsu: first recombinant plasmid transformed into E. coli BIOTECH ★
Combined restriction digestion, ligation and CaCl₂ transformation to clone foreign DNA into pSC101 — the founding experiment of genetic engineering, only possible because artificial transformation now existed.
DOI: 10.1073/pnas.70.11.3240 · PubMed record
1983 — Hanahan's optimized supercompetent strains (DH5α-era protocols) EFFICIENCY ★
Systematically varied strain genotype, growth phase, buffers, DMSO and divalent cations to reach ~10⁸–10⁹ transformants/µg — up to 100× CaCl₂-only efficiencies. Showed that the strain's genotype is a key variable of DNA entry efficiency.
DOI: 10.1016/S0022-2836(83)80284-8 · PubMed record
1988 — Dower, Miller & Ragsdale: electroporation of E. coli at ~10¹⁰/µg METHOD ★
Brief high-voltage exponential pulses create reversible aqueous pores in the membrane; DNA enters directly through these dielectric breakdown pores. Transformation of strains "refractory" to chemical methods became possible.
DOI: 10.1093/nar/16.13.6001 · PubMed record
2004 — Chen & Dubnau: the molecular machine of natural DNA uptake REVIEW ★
Synthesized the genetics of natural competence: a type IV pilus-like pseudopilus pulls double-stranded DNA to the cell surface, a membrane receptor (ComEA/ComW) binds it, a nuclease (EndA/NucA) degrades one strand, and a ComEC transmembrane channel imports single-stranded DNA — driven by the ComFA ATPase.
DOI: 10.1038/nrmicro844 · PubMed record · Nat Rev Microbiol 2:241–249

How DNA Actually Moves In — and How Mutants Revealed It

Comparing easily-transformed vs poorly-transformed strains was the key genetic strategy: knock out a gene, see whether DNA entry drops, and you have a component of the uptake machine. This is how nearly every protein below was found.

1. Natural competence (pneumococcus, B. subtilis, H. influenzae, V. cholerae, Acinetobacter…)

Step 1 — Surface capture. A dynamic type IV pilus / pseudopilus (ComGC pilins in Gram-positives; PilA-like proteins in Gram-negatives) extends and retracts, dragging dsDNA to the surface. Non-competent mutants (e.g. comGC knockouts) cannot bind DNA at all → identified the pilus as the first gate.
Step 2 — Receptor binding. ComEA binds dsDNA outside the membrane. In H. influenzae and N. gonorrhoeae a specific uptake sequence (USS: 5'-AAGTGCGGT-3' or DUS: 5'-GCCGTCTGAA-3') in the DNA is recognized → these species take up their own DNA preferentially. mutS/uptake-motif mutants lose specificity.
Step 3 — One strand destroyed. EndA (pneumococcus) or NucA (B. subtilis) nicks and degrades one strand at the membrane; the degraded strand's nucleotides even serve as a signal to keep competence going. endA mutants bind DNA but import almost nothing → proved ssDNA is the transported species.
Step 4 — Translocation. ComEC forms the inner-membrane channel; only one strand passes through, powered by the ComFA ATPase. comEC mutants are DNA-binding-proficient but transformation-deficient — the definitive "channel" mutant. Then RecA integrates the ssDNA by homologous recombination.

2. Regulation — why cells are competent only sometimes

Competence is a starvation/stress program: quorum-sensing peptides (ComX in B. subtilis, CSP in pneumococcus) trigger a phosphorelay → ComK or ComE transcription factors switch on all com genes at once. Cells defective in the circuit (e.g. comQ/comP sensor mutants) are hard-to-transform; strains engineered to express ComK constitutively or pre-grown with CSP become easy-to-transform. This regulatory genetics is exactly why "the same species" can be easy or hard depending on the strain and the protocol.

3. Artificial entry (E. coli-style) — what the mutants taught us

CaCl₂/heat-shock and electroporation bypass all natural competence genes — that is why E. coli, which is not naturally competent, can still be transformed. But the efficiency of entry still depends on the cell envelope genotype (next tab): restriction systems destroy incoming DNA after entry, methylation states determine whether the host recognizes the DNA as "self", and envelope mutations alter membrane permeability.

Take-home logic: "easy entry" strains = entry barrier minimized (permeabilization works) + post-entry defenses deleted (hsdR, mcr, recA) + recovery maximized (rich recovery medium, no restriction). "Hard" strains still carry one or more of these barriers — and each hard strain, once systematically mutated, is exactly how the individual barriers were discovered.

Getting DNA into Eukaryotic Cells — the Methods and Their Discovery Papers

Calcium phosphate co-precipitation (1973) CLASSIC

DNA mixed with CaCl₂ and phosphate buffer forms DNA–CaPO₄ microcrystals; cells engulf them by endocytosis/phagocytosis. First widely used method for mammalian cells — showed that a simple inorganic co-precipitate could carry genes into cultured cells.

DOI: 10.1016/0042-6822(73)90341-3 · PubMed record
DEAE-dextran (1968) TRANSIENT

A positively charged polymer complexes with DNA and delivers it into cells via endocytosis — mostly for transient expression, very simple, low toxicity control needed.

DOI: 10.1084/jcb.47.3.688 · PubMed record
Lipofection (cationic lipids) (1987) MOST USED ★

Synthetic cationic lipids (DOTMA, later Lipofectamine-class reagents) form liposomes that fuse with the plasma membrane and deliver DNA directly into the cytoplasm. The paper that launched the entire field of non-viral DNA delivery reagents.

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.84.21.7413 · PubMed: 2823261
Polyethylenimine (PEI) (1995) HIGH CHARGE

A highly cationic polymer condenses DNA into "polyplexes" that enter by endocytosis; the "proton-sponge" effect bursts endosomes and frees DNA. Workhorse for both cell culture and, importantly, in vivo delivery.

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.92.16.7297 · PubMed record
Electroporation of eukaryotes (1982) ROBUST ★

Short high-voltage pulses open transient membrane pores; DNA diffuses in before pores reseal. Works on cells refractory to all chemical methods, including primary cells and T cells (basis of modern CAR-T engineering).

DOI: 10.1002/j.1460-2075.1982.tb01257.x · PubMed record
Microinjection (1980) PRECISION ★

Glass micropipette injects DNA straight into the nucleus/cytoplasm of a single cell — 100% delivery to the chosen cell; the technique behind transgenic mice and the Nobel-recognized work on gene targeting.

DOI: 10.1016/0092-8674(80)90359-3 · PubMed record
Biolistics / gene gun (1990) TISSUES

Gold microparticles coated with DNA are accelerated by a helium pulse into cells and even into tissue in vivo — including plant cells with walls that resist every other method. DNA literally shot through the membrane.

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.87.24.9568 · PubMed record
Nucleofection (2000s) PRIMARY CELLS

Combines electroporation programs with cell-type-specific buffers so DNA is delivered directly into the nucleus — solved the "hard" primary cells and stem cells that chemical transfection barely touches.

DOI: 10.1634/stemcells.22-5-622 · PubMed record
Retroviral vectors (1983) HIGH EFFICIENCY ★

Engineered Moloney murine leukemia viruses carry the therapeutic gene into the genome of dividing cells — the first safe, replication-defective viral delivery system, later the basis of Strimvelis and early gene therapy trials.

DOI: 10.1016/0092-8674(83)90236-1 · PubMed record
Lentiviral vectors (1996) WORKHORSE ★

HIV-derived vectors transduce non-dividing cells (neurons, stem cells, T cells) and integrate stably — the delivery system behind ex vivo CAR-T cell therapy.

DOI: 10.1126/science.272.5259.263 · PubMed record
AAV vectors (1989) IN VIVO ★

Adeno-associated virus vectors transduce muscle, liver, retina and CNS with low immunogenicity — the vector class of modern in vivo gene therapy (Luxturna, Zolgensma, Hemgenix).

DOI: 10.1126/science.2462430 · PubMed record
Adenoviral vectors HIGH TITER

High-titer, broad tropism, non-integrating vectors for transient high expression, used in vaccines and oncolytic virotherapy.

DOI: 10.1038/sj.gt.3301685 · PubMed record
